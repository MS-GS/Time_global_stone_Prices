Reviewed 15 November 2026. This is an editorial source record, not a quotation or a material test certificate.

## Content approach

The source catalogue supplies colour/trade names, photos, sizes and prices. It does not establish the mineral identity of the white, black, maroon, rainbow or green products. Their descriptions therefore stay with decorative colour and design uses. Photos and trade names cannot establish aquarium safety, frost resistance, load capacity, filtration performance, chemical inertness or fire resistance.

## Primary references

1. **Royal Horticultural Society — Gravel gardens**
   https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/for-places/gravel-gardens
   Supports ornamental gravel gardens and coordinating colours with paving. Medium angular grades are more comfortable to walk on than rounded gravel/cobbles. Decorative pebbles should not automatically be sold as a stable walking surface. Limestone may conflict with acid-loving plants. Establishing a gravel garden still involves weeding and suitable soil conditions.

2. **University of Maryland Extension — Mulching Trees and Shrubs**
   https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/mulching-trees-and-shrubs
   Discusses lasting stone and lava mulch in rock/alpine gardens and the lower weight of lava relative to gravel. Warns that limestone can alter pH. This is general horticultural evidence, not a certification for the supplied stones.

3. **Ask Extension — What about Pumice? #779486**
   https://ask.extension.org/kb/faq.php?id=779486
   Expert advice describes pumice as lightweight volcanic rock used to assist aeration and drainage in potting mixes. It holds some water and supplies no nutrients. Product grading and the full growing mix matter; the catalogue's 1–2 cm pieces should not be described as universally suitable for every plant or growing medium.

4. **Iowa State University Extension — Using Mulch in the Garden**
   https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/using-mulch-garden
   Lists river rock, lava rock and pea gravel among inorganic mulches. They do not improve soil fertility or structure and can retain excessive summer heat. Weeds can still develop in rock cover. Avoid universal claims such as maintenance-free or weed-proof.

5. **Missouri Department of Natural Resources — Granite PUB2906**
   https://dnr.mo.gov/document-search/granite-pub2906/pub2906
   Explains granite variation and its broader ornamental, architectural and aggregate applications. Such generic uses do not establish that this catalogue's loose decorative granite is specified for paving, structural work or cladding.

6. **US Geological Survey — Building America: USGS and the Nation's Construction Materials**
   https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/geology-energy-minerals/science/building-america-usgs-and-nations-construction
   Distinguishes aggregate construction uses generally. No performance guarantee should be derived for a supplier's untested decorative grade.

### Price units confirmed by the business owner

The business owner confirmed that **every listed price is USD per metric tonne (1,000 kg)**, including the middle column for stone supplied in 18 kg packs. The 18 kg figure describes packing, not the price unit. The new catalogue explicitly states USD per tonne across all three options. A quotation confirms packing, grade, quantity, taxes and transport. Preserve the business's 27 tonne international minimum and ex-factory/VAT-excluded terms from the supplied page; web research does not independently verify those business terms.

## General care and selection copy

Natural colour and appearance can vary between batches and when wet. Request a sample where matching matters. Choose size, layer depth, containment and sub-base for the intended installation; pebbles alone do not fix poor site drainage. Keep decorative landscape guidance separate from aquarium, structural, filtration, firepit or food-contact suitability, all of which need explicit product-specific confirmation.
